## Dimensions at the work depth

The California field study measured a 230 m crossbeam perimeter at 125 m on Eureka and a 232 m perimeter at 115 m on Gail. At its nominal 126 m working level, Kestrel has a roughly 60 by 54 m footprint and a 227 m perimeter. Its steel continues above and below the diver. The prototype's 23 m vertical bays, splayed eight-leg layout, 5.94 m corner sleeve and narrower internal conductors are design choices. Both work orders fit on a small part of the near face. [Love et al., table 1](https://lovelab.msi.ucsb.edu/Love-et-al-2019-Eureka-Gail.pdf#page=3)

## Visibility at Kestrel

The dark water, short range and density of marine snow are atmosphere choices for this particular watch. A swept helmet light reveals a few metres of crust and steel at a time. Silts, ragged flakes and occasional longer threads drift in different layers, so a passing fleck crosses the eye faster than a distant one. With the helmet lamp off, a few indicator lenses and the bell windows remain visible. The reference photographs are linked for study. All surfaces and geometry shipped in the game are original to the project.
