# Underwater

## The first watch

The cook has put two pieces of toast in the food lock, buttered face to face so the butter will melt. Ellis asks if you want yours now or after the run. Much later you will remember him asking this because it is the last thing on the tape before you hear somebody else finish your shift.

You are the only diver in the water on this bell run. You work the east jacket of Kestrel, a fictional fixed oil production platform in the northern North Sea. The bell hangs beside a bay of steel at a nominal depth of 126 metres. The surface and the bottom are both out of sight. Look up and a tube the width of a room disappears through green water. Look down and exactly the same thing happens. It is possible to become fond of a particular patch of yellow paint here.

An ordinary watch should be worth playing twice. An observant player should gradually become reluctant to let another diver take their place. The proposed full game spans one tour in saturation. The first playable build covers one repeatable bell run. All names, records and incidents are invented.

## A working day at pressure

The home of this crew is the saturation system aboard a diving support vessel alongside the installation. Chamber and closed bell connect under pressure. The crew can ride back onto the vessel, eat a hot meal and go to bed, while still living at storage pressure. Eventual decompression takes days. This gives the story a particularly uncomfortable kind of distance. There are people on the other side of a steel wall who can pass you a book but cannot open the door and shake your hand. [John Osborne's first-person account](https://www.aquamarinemedicals.co.uk/resources/life-as-a-saturation-diver)

1. **Wake and handover.** The outgoing team leaves a drawing, the latest readings and small practical advice. Meals and kit pass through locks. Before a bell run, the supervisor and bellman go through their checks. A half-finished remark about a fitting can be more important to the incoming diver than an entire tidy report. [Osborne](https://www.aquamarinemedicals.co.uk/resources/life-as-a-saturation-diver)
2. **One diver out.** You and Ellis ride in a two-person bell. Ellis stays dry, tends your umbilical and stands by for rescue. The line carries breathing gas, hot water and communications. Noor supervises from the ship. Mara is on the relief watch and leaves notes on the same steel you inspect. Once you round a leg, your lamp can be the only light you see. The UK offshore code allows up to four hours in the water per diver on a two-person bell run. [UK offshore diving code, paragraphs 97–98 and 104](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/l103.pdf#page=23)
3. **Earn the photograph.** Real offshore work includes identifying the specified joint, inspecting welds, taking photographs, checking wall thickness and installing mechanical fittings. The operator uses inspection plans to pick particular stress points. A clear reading from the wrong member is useless. The player performs the work on the structure and leaves a record tied to each inspection location. Kestrel’s instruments and work orders are fictional. [Maersk, Ensuring Production, pp. 11–12](https://www.maersk.com.cn/~/media_sc9/maersk/corporate/press/publications/files/2014-october-maersk-post-full-issue.pdf) · [Boskalis](https://magazine.boskalis.com/issue07/subsea) · [North Sea clamp and guide design](https://deesubsea.co.uk/clamp-design/)
4. **Come back through the same hole.** Kit, the bellman's hands and a debrief await at the bell. On the vessel the living system has bunks and space to wash and eat. Divers interviewed about saturation describe camaraderie, boredom, reading, films and the importance of a private routine. Model little intervals of domestic life. One crew member always rewinds the tape when somebody comes in halfway through a film. A repeated scene on the television can be perfectly innocent. Actual schedules vary by operation. [Osborne](https://www.aquamarinemedicals.co.uk/resources/life-as-a-saturation-diver) · [Interview study with saturation divers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2022.765197/full)

## The playable contract

The opening view is a suspended platform jacket and a working copy of the dive sheet. Start at the bell. Take either docket. Read the bay code, leave the bell and swim along the steel. Swim within reach of the matching fixture and pick up the tool. Turning your head, moving closer, and backing away stay in your hands throughout the job. Your helmet lamp follows where you look. Finish the local work and swim to the next marked position. A playable docket aims for five to ten minutes of inspection or fitting work. File the finished series, find the bell and hand it in. Take the other job or return on another watch. Nothing expires while the diver looks around.

| Docket | What the hands do | What the eye learns | Satisfaction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A2 · Wall survey | Check the gauge against a known block, clean each scheduled test location, seat the contact probe and record a stable thickness. Lift and re-seat to confirm a low reading. | Where marine growth still prevents contact and where the sleeve has lost metal. | A location-by-location thickness report that the relief can reopen. |
| B4 · Service-line guides | Clean the saddle and offer the lined guide around the service line. Start the fasteners, draw each side down progressively, finish with the preset wrench and check both mating edges. | How loose hardware shifts, and how a guide sits when the two sides draw down evenly. | A visibly finished support and a record of the fitting checks. |

The survey follows the instrument maker’s contact, stability and verification workflow. The clamp job draws on documented diver-installed offshore supports. Kestrel’s inspection plan, measurements, hardware and acceptance values belong to this fictional installation. [Cygnus DIVE operating manual](https://cygnus-instruments.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/M3-DIVE-M-ENG_Iss11.pdf) · [Forties Alpha riser-support installation](https://www.lindapter.com/us/case-study/aviat-gas-field-development)

A diver can leave the tool and keep exploring. Nobody grades speed. A fresh watch can revisit the same patch with different survey readings and fit-up conditions. Later bays should introduce new ways to read familiar equipment before they introduce new controls.

In the larger game a cleaned identification plate stays legible, a fitted carrier holds a lamp where you left it, and an older survey can be overlaid on a fresh reading. The rewards are better visibility, a familiar route, and confidence in your own record. Keep the first hours free of a money grind.

## The place and the crew

Build one vertical neighbourhood. On the east face, horizontal service bars carry paired cable runs and tags. Diagonals make different shapes at each intersection. The bell is the only large amber body in the water. Its downline goes straight up. The diver's excursion stays close to the structure and the working level. Neutral buoyancy arrests drift when input stops. In the full game a bell move stages a new working level. A long swim upward cannot take the place of a transfer under pressure.

Noor likes exact member IDs. She gives a bearing with reference to a fixture the diver can see. Ellis remembers who likes the jam left on the side of the plate. Mara is an experienced relief diver with a habit of filing one corner off her survey shims so she can identify them by touch. She is the first person prepared to say she has seen something and the last prepared to guess what it means. The relief is someone the player can come to care for before the last handover. No member of the crew needs to be secretly malicious.

Give the chamber equal weight in the full game. Let the player label a tape, dry their cuffs over a familiar rail, add a sketch to the job book, or pull the thin curtain at their bunk. Meals come through the lock from an unseen cook. Let a whole visit pass with nothing uncanny. On the vessel time is marked by people doing things for one another.

## Story proposal · the relief face

Keep the following out of the first demo apart from a small optional note and the deniable seeds described under *Dread with an explanation*. The title of the game and the dive sheet should continue to sound like ordinary work.

**A discrepancy small enough to sign.** A weld photo from the prior shift shows a carrier just outside frame. You can find its shadow and its two clean witness marks, though the carrier itself is in your tool basket. Ellis says they probably lifted it between the photo and the handover. Most players will keep working. Those who manually compare their own photograph with the prior one will notice the file mark on Mara's shim has changed sides.

**The voluntary turn.** At supper Mara offers the other half of a stereo recording. Take it to the bench if you want. Align the two reels using the double knock of a basket landing. The free channel carries another crew working a few seconds beyond the end of your tape. This discovery unlocks a second layer on the inspection drawing. Nobody puts it on the job list.

**Learn a rule.** Only work that has been witnessed and signed for appears on the second face. An unrecorded chalk line stays singular. Mark a carrier, photograph it, then visit the apparently empty bay indicated by the second drawing. There is your mark, on a carrier with years of accumulated wear. Look back through the braces and your real work light is still on. The player can repeat this with an ordinary test piece. There is concrete evidence for the rule and considerable room for disagreement about the cause.

**What the older hands did.** Several generations of relief notes quietly pair real joint IDs with other positions. Some corrections kept workers from searching empty water. Others sent a diver to touch a fitting which had only ever appeared on paper. Older crews had already worked out that a diver can briefly occupy both records at handover. Their solution was to leave directions for the next person. The institution has kept the directions while forgetting what they were for.

**Last handover.** The outgoing player's name is already on a completed sheet in the relief's handwriting. You can leave the factual diagram and a personal instruction in the margin, or leave the diagram alone. In the chamber, listen to your relief do the first job from the game. If you left a note they recognise the misplaced carrier and call it by its proper name. If you didn't, they spend a long time describing it to a patient Ellis. Both sign the sheet. From inside the closed food lock, in your own voice, comes the first half of the next handover. Cut on the sound of the dog turning. The relief's log remains playable for ordinary watches afterward.

The player can pass on understanding and spare the next diver one bewildering experience. The process that reproduces the work and the worker continues. Leave open whether the archive has been making another platform, the second platform has been dictating the archive, or a person living at pressure simply moves between the two.

## Dread with an explanation

The fear in this game comes from the place, not from a threat. A diver alone at 126 metres is already in a frightening situation, so the game only has to stop reassuring them. Everything strange in the water has an ordinary explanation, and somebody on the crew gives it in a calm voice. The player decides whether to believe it. Nothing approaches, chases, grades or hurts the diver. The docket never changes because of something the diver saw.

Four rules keep it honest.

1. **Quiet first.** The first half minute outside is only the regulator, the jacket's hum and Ellis. Atmosphere is earned by ordinary time in the water.
2. **Warmth makes the cold.** Ellis is kind and specific before anything goes wrong. A man keeping your toast warm is what makes a silent hardline unbearable.
3. **One odd thing at a time.** Leave at least twenty seconds between beats. Let each one resolve completely. The lamp always comes back. The fish always return.
4. **The explanation is plausible and slightly wrong.** The crew explains with what they can see from the bell. The diver can see a little more.

### The first watch, beat by beat

| On the watch clock | What happens | What the crew says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lock-out | Black water outside the wet porch. The hatch, then the regulator. Ellis's voice arrives before the eyes adjust. The view fades in over about three seconds. | "Diver's out. I have your line." The bearing follows once the diver can see. |
| ~40 s | Nothing at all happens. | Ellis mentions the toast on the heater, butter side in. |
| ~80 s | A long structural groan rises from far below. The jacket's machinery hum drops out for a while. A pressure wave jolts the helmet picture. The shoal bolts from the light and stays away for about a minute. The diver's breathing quickens and takes half a minute to settle. | "That's the jacket taking the tide. She talks when it turns." |
| ~150 s | The helmet lamp sputters and dies for three seconds. In the dark a single cold work light shows deep inside the jacket, in a bay with no docket, beyond the reach of the umbilical. The lamp comes back on the breaker. The far light stays lit a moment longer, then goes out. | "Lost your lamp on the panel for a second. Breaker's reset itself." |
| ~215 s | Static on the hardline. A thin, broken voice that belongs to nobody repeats Ellis's first words about your station. | If the diver calls the bell: "That wasn't me. Hardline's been quiet a good minute." |
| Next swim through open water | Something long and pale slides across the far edge of the lamp under the bell. Held in the beam, it turns away and sinks out of sight. No sound marks it. | If the diver calls the bell: "A sleeper, maybe. They come up to the lights. It won't bother you." |

After the groan, the water talks occasionally on its own. There are single dull knocks, a chain dragging somewhere overhead, and the tick of cold steel. Rarely there is a double knock, like a basket landing, which always comes from the bay with the far light. These sounds are muffled through water and give only a vague direction, as they do for a real diver.

A diver who sits in the dark with their lamp off will find the far light again after a few seconds. Backscatter from their own lamp hides a faint light that far away, which is true underwater and is the in-world reason the light only shows in darkness. If the diver calls the bell while looking at it, Ellis says nobody has rigged lights in that bay and that it is probably their own lamp off a guide plate. Ellis does not know the diver's lamp is off. Sitting in the dark for long enough brings Noor on the line: "Your picture's gone black up here. Lamp?" Someone is always watching the helmet feed.

Later watches move sooner and explain less. The tide line becomes "You know her by now." The lamp fault goes in the book for the technicians. The crosstalk repeats something Ellis said to you about your toast. On the third watch it asks Bell 2 to confirm two divers on the jacket. Each is still deniable as a crossed line or another vessel's tape.

These are seeds for *the relief face*, not its rules. The far light is where the second drawing will later send the diver. The double knock is the sound used to align Mara's reels. The crosstalk is the other crew a few seconds past the end of the tape. A first-time player should be able to finish both dockets, hear every beat and still describe the watch as a normal day at work with a bad lamp.

## Picture, controls and sound

The excursion begins at 126 metres in nearly black water. The helmet lamp strikes a single arc of a leg thick enough to hide the bell behind it. A diagonal continues past the edge of the light. A ladder rung, a hose clip and the diver’s hand establish scale. Grain sits down near the blacks. The edge of the faceplate mists faintly with every exhale and clears on the next draw, so a frightened diver sees their own breathing quicken. Suspended flakes turn through the beam at different distances. Fish passing between the braces briefly catch the lamp, then disappear into open water. Switch off the lamp and distant bell lights are among the few things still visible. A fine CRT scan and slight colour bleed give the helmet view the texture of an older signal. It is the same feed Noor watches from the ship. When the hardline crackles or the jacket moves, the signal tears for a moment. Keep the tooling legible through it. Shape the jacket from the underwater photographs in [the reference notebook](docs/underwater-references.md).

Keep the paperwork at the bell. A thumb-stained sheet carries the short job list and the ink left by the previous watch. In the water a caption can carry a voice, then fade. Each piece of equipment carries its own controls. The ultrasonic gauge shows a live echo when the probe seats on steel. The two clamp halves and their captive fasteners sit on the actual service line.

Landscape phone leads. Swim with the left thumb and look with the right. Swim into arm’s reach and use a free thumb directly on the equipment. The diver can swim or turn to look into open water during any step. Desktop players work with the left mouse button as they aim the helmet. The bellman tends the line and gives bearings over the hardline. Save signed work and instrument settings aboard the device.

Use the Home Twenty method of generating named ElevenLabs sound effects at authoring time, shaping and looping locally, then shipping audio with the build. Outside the bell, the regulator has an ordinary working rhythm. Air flows in smoothly, the exhaust is softer, and there is a little silence before the next draw. Over a long job it should be easy to forget the diver is breathing. The contact of a tool still comes through clearly. Let the sound of good contact be short enough that a diver enjoys making it twice. A far-off knock can be part of the structure settling. Sounds from out in the structure go through a muffled, very large space and give only a rough direction. After a big sound the jacket's own hum falls silent for several seconds, and the silence should be the frightening part. Faster breathing is the only alarm the game uses. Save music for a time when removing it can change the room.

## Scope and playtest

The slice tests leaving the bell, finding two nearby fixtures, doing two kinds of skilled work, reading feedback, and handing over. It can suggest a larger crew through written comms and a prior-watch note. Chamber exploration, a campaign, the second face, recorded dialogue and the ending belong to subsequent builds. No timer, oxygen economy, enemy or forced pursuit belongs in this prototype. The animal at the edge of the light is an animal, and it always leaves.

For a first phone test, hand someone the bell sheet without explaining the controls. Watch where they look for A2, whether they can tell a bad echo from a good one after one correction, and whether they choose the other docket after handing in the first. Ask which landmark they used on the swim home and what they thought the relief meant. Ask whether they turned the lamp off on purpose after it failed, whether they noticed the light in the far bay, and whether they wanted to swim to it. A player who pauses a job to stare into the dark has found the atmosphere. A player who stops working altogether means it is too loud. A person who enjoyed the carrier but missed the mystery is a successful first watch. A person who understood the story but would skip the next job means the tools need more work.
